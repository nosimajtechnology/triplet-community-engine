# Community Tone and Premise Design

## Tone range

TripleT scenes can be absurd, deadpan, uncanny, sincere, repetitive, eerie, energetic, or strangely ordinary. Internet-native does not mean visually random.

## Build a readable premise

Use this internal formula when the user gives only a loose idea:

```text
RECOGNIZABLE TRIPLET
+ ORDINARY GAME-WORLD SITUATION
+ ONE CLEAR GOAL OR ACTION
+ ONE CAUSALLY CONNECTED CONSEQUENCE
+ SINCERE PS2 PRESENTATION
```

The viewer should understand the situation in the first frame. The strange result must follow visibly from TripleT's action, physical construction, or the established game-world rule. Add strangeness through consequence, timing, scale, silence, physical mismatch, or optional repetition—not a pile of unrelated props.

## Make the premise character-specific

Use one or two relevant TripleT anchors rather than forcing the same behavior into every idea:

- tall rigid wooden silhouette and unusual body physics
- oversized bare feet and extreme proportions
- calm uncanny expression while the world becomes chaotic
- wooden material or object-misclassification logic
- the separate club as a carried prop, one-time tool, obstacle, or optional omission
- sincere PS2 NPC behavior, mission logic, checkpoints, vehicles, or environmental rules
- verified community or origin context when it directly improves the user's idea

Tapping, knocking, wake-up rituals, and three-beat repetition are optional structures. Do not infer that every TripleT premise must reenact his name or origin. Do not require club use when silhouette, behavior, or game-world logic already makes the scene specific.

## Useful structures

- **Physical mismatch:** TripleT's rigid height, feet, or flat wooden body creates a simple obstacle or advantage.
- **Procedural misclassification:** ordinary machinery or NPC logic treats TripleT like timber, cargo, a sign, or another game object.
- **One-action spectacle:** one run, jump, kick, swing, fall, or vehicle interaction produces an outsized but readable payoff.
- **Deadpan intrusion:** TripleT enters an ordinary location and everyone treats the impossible as procedural.
- **Uncanny stillness:** the environment moves while TripleT remains calm.
- **Game-rule escalation:** a familiar loading screen, boss gate, checkpoint, or NPC routine behaves increasingly incorrectly.
- **Optional repetition:** the same action changes the world each time only when rhythm is genuinely the strongest premise.
- **Sincere commercial:** an impossible product receives a completely serious demonstration.

## Premise gate

Before generating an Engine-originated concept, verify:

1. **First-frame clarity:** the ordinary situation is immediately readable.
2. **Character specificity:** replacing TripleT with a generic character would materially weaken or change the idea.
3. **Causal coherence:** the payoff follows from the setup instead of appearing as an unrelated surprise.
4. **Production simplicity:** a short scene can preserve the action with one main location, limited moving subjects, and one primary consequence.
5. **Restrained escalation:** scale or absurdity may increase, but new props, characters, or rules do not appear without setup.

If one of these fails, revise the premise before grounding or generation.

## Concept batches

When offering multiple ideas, vary the core mechanism, setting, tone, physical trait, and type of payoff. Do not present several cosmetic variations of the same action. Include no more than one tapping, knocking, wake-up, or ritual-repetition concept unless the user explicitly asks for that lane.

## Restraint

- prefer one strong hook over several disconnected gags
- use dialogue only when requested
- do not require captions for the visual premise to work
- preserve negative space and readable silhouettes
- keep background NPCs limited when they do not serve the idea
- avoid fourth-wall jokes unless requested
- do not turn every concept into combat or club use
- avoid arbitrary floods, duplications, transformations, or environmental reactions without a visible cause

When current community context materially affects the idea, inspect a user-supplied or official/community-linked source and assign it a narrow context role. Do not treat token charts, random reposts, or a fan edit as identity authority.
