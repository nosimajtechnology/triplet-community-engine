# Community Tone and Premise Design

## Tone range

TripleT scenes can be absurd, deadpan, uncanny, sincere, repetitive, eerie, energetic, or strangely ordinary. Internet-native does not mean visually random.

## Build a readable premise

Use this internal formula when the user gives only a loose idea:

```text
RECOGNIZABLE TRIPLET
+ ORDINARY GAME-WORLD SITUATION
+ ONE CLEAR ACTION OR REPETITION
+ ONE ESCALATING CONSEQUENCE
+ SINCERE PS2 PRESENTATION
```

The viewer should understand the situation in the first frame. Add strangeness through consequence, timing, scale, silence, or repetition—not a pile of unrelated props.

## Useful structures

- **Deadpan intrusion:** TripleT enters an ordinary location and everyone treats the impossible as procedural.
- **Three-beat repetition:** the same tap, knock, step, or glance changes the world each time.
- **Uncanny stillness:** the environment moves while TripleT remains calm.
- **Game-rule escalation:** a familiar loading screen, boss gate, checkpoint, or NPC routine behaves increasingly incorrectly.
- **Sincere commercial:** an impossible product receives a completely serious demonstration.

## Restraint

- prefer one strong hook over several disconnected gags
- use dialogue only when requested
- do not require captions for the visual premise to work
- preserve negative space and readable silhouettes
- keep background NPCs limited when they do not serve the idea
- avoid fourth-wall jokes unless requested
- do not turn every concept into combat or club use

When current community context materially affects the idea, inspect a user-supplied or official/community-linked source and assign it a narrow context role. Do not treat token charts, random reposts, or a fan edit as identity authority.
